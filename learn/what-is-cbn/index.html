What Is CBN?

November 2, 20263 min readBy Uforia Dispensary

CBN is the cannabinoid that time makes. Rather than coming mostly from the growing plant, it forms gradually as THC ages, which gives it an unusual place in the cannabis story.

On this page

  1. A cannabinoid that forms with age
  2. How it compares with THC
  3. What people say about CBN
  4. Storing flower to slow the change

A cannabinoid that forms with age

CBN, short for cannabinol, was one of the first cannabinoids scientists isolated, in the late 1800s. Unlike THC or CBD, it is not something the plant produces in large amounts. Instead, CBN forms as THC breaks down. Researchers first isolated it from samples of cannabis resin, decades before THC itself was identified in the 1960s.

A living plant makes THCA, which heat or time turns into THC. Our THCA guide explains that step. When THC is exposed to oxygen, light and warmth over a long stretch, it slowly oxidizes, and some of it becomes CBN. That is why older flower, or flower kept somewhere warm and bright, tends to test higher in CBN and lower in THC. How quickly this happens depends on storage: flower kept cool and dark changes far more slowly.

How it compares with THC

CBN is generally described as far milder than THC. It appears to bind only weakly to the CB1 receptors that THC acts on, so on its own it is not known for a strong high. Products that feature CBN often contain THC as well, though, so the overall experience depends on the full mix of compounds.

You may also see CBN listed by name on products where it has been added deliberately, often in blends with THC or CBD rather than on its own.

What people say about CBN

CBN has a reputation as the "sleepy" cannabinoid. You will often see it in products marketed for winding down in the evening, and some people say aged cannabis feels heavier than fresh.

The research has not caught up with the reputation. Studies of CBN in people are few and small, and the results so far are mixed. Some researchers suspect the drowsy feeling linked to older cannabis may have as much to do with THC and terpenes as with CBN itself. How any product feels also depends on the amount, the other compounds in it and your own tolerance. Until more research is done, it is best to see CBN as an interesting compound rather than a promise of any particular result.

Storing flower to slow the change

Whether you are curious about CBN or simply want your flower to stay fresh, storage makes a difference. Light and air do most of the damage, and heat speeds things up. To slow the breakdown of THC:

  • Keep flower in an airtight glass container.
  • Leave it out of the fridge, where moisture and temperature swings can affect quality.
  • Store it somewhere cool and dark, away from windows, stoves and other heat sources.
  • Open the container only when you need to, since each opening lets in fresh air.

For more on enjoying cannabis thoughtfully, visit our safe consumption page. If you try a product with CBN, start low and go slow. When you are ready to explore, browse Uforia's live menu of edibles and tinctures.

Summary

Key takeaways

  • CBN forms as THC ages and oxidizes, so older flower tends to contain more of it.
  • It is generally described as much milder than THC.
  • Its sleepy reputation is popular, but research in people is limited and mixed.
  • Cool, dark, airtight storage slows the breakdown of THC.
